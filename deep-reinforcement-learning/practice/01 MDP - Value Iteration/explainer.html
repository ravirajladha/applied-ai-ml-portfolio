🧊 Frozen Lake, explained like you're new to this

No jargon. We'll build up the whole idea — the problem, the setup, "values", and the "policy" — one small piece at a time, with a grid you can actually click.

1. The problem (the story) 🎁

Imagine a little robot standing on a frozen lake made of square tiles. Somewhere on the lake there's a gift 🎁. The robot wants to walk to the gift.

But some tiles are holes 💧. Step on a hole and the robot falls in — game over, no gift.

🧠 Relate it to: it's like a tiny board game. You want to walk from Start to the 🎁, avoiding the 💧 traps. That's literally the whole problem. Everything else we do is just teaching the robot the smartest path.

The robot earns +1 point only when it reaches the gift. Stepping on a normal tile = 0 points. So the robot's mission: collect that 1 point by reaching the gift safely.

2. The setup (the board) 🗺️

Here's our lake. 4 rows × 4 columns = 16 tiles. Each tile has a number (0 to 15) so the computer can talk about it.

Frozen (safe) 💧 Hole (trap) 🎁 Gift (goal) Start
🧠 "Setup" just means: tell the computer what the board looks like — where the start, holes, and gift are. In the code that's the line gym.make('FrozenLake-v1'). It hands you this exact board.

3. The 5 words people use (MDP) 🧩

"MDP" is just a fancy name for a game with these 5 ingredients. You already understand all of them — here are the plain-English versions:

Fancy wordWhat it really meansOn our lake
StateWhere you are right nowWhich tile you're standing on (0–15)
ActionA move you can makeStep Left, Down, Right, or Up
TransitionWhere a move takes youStep Right from tile 0 → tile 1
RewardPoints for what just happened+1 for reaching 🎁, else 0
Discount (γ)"Sooner is better than later"A number like 0.9 (more on this below)
🧠 Discount (γ) in one line: winning now feels better than winning in 10 steps. γ = 0.9 means "each step of waiting shrinks the prize a little" — it nudges the robot to find the shortest safe path, not just any path.

4. "Value" — how good is each tile? 🔥❄️ (click me!)

Here's the key idea. We want to give every tile a score that answers: "If I'm standing here and play smart, how close am I to that gift?" We call that score the tile's value.

🧠 Think of the hot-and-cold game. The gift tile is hot 🔥. Tiles right next to it are pretty hot. Tiles far away are cold ❄️. The "value" is exactly this hotness — and it spreads outward from the gift, one ripple at a time.

Press "Do one step" and watch the heat spread out from the gift. This process is called value iteration — literally "figure out the values, step by step".

All tiles start cold (value 0). Press “Do one step”.
🧠 What one "step" does: for every tile, the robot asks "of my 4 possible moves, which one lands me on the best (hottest) neighbour?" and copies a bit of that heat onto itself. Repeat a few times and the heat reaches all the way back to the Start. That's the whole trick. ✨

5. "Policy" — the cheat-sheet of arrows 🧭

Once every tile has a value, the smart move on any tile is easy: step toward your hottest neighbour. If you draw that best move as an arrow on every tile, you get a policy.

🧠 A policy is just GPS directions. "On this tile, go this way." Follow the arrows from Start and they walk you around every hole, straight to the 🎁. Press "Show the arrows" above to see it.

In the notebook, the function extract_policy() does exactly this: it looks at the values and writes down the best arrow for each tile.

6. Putting the words together 🔁

  1. Problem: get the robot from Start to the 🎁 without falling in a 💧.
  2. Setup: load the board — gym.make('FrozenLake-v1').
  3. Value iteration: spread "hotness" out from the gift until every tile has a value (how good it is).
  4. Policy extraction: on each tile, draw an arrow toward the hottest neighbour. Follow the arrows → you win.
🎯 That's the entire notebook. "Value iteration" fills in the heat map; "extract policy" turns the heat map into arrows. Everything else is just Python plumbing around these two ideas.
