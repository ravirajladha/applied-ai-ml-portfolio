2 · Bi-LSTM-CRF — click through the stack

Concept: combine a powerful feature extractor (Bi-LSTM) with a structured prediction layer (CRF) to ensure valid tag sequences.

Typical accuracy~97–98% on the Penn Treebank
ProsHighly accurate · automatically learns features · good for low-resource languages (with transfer learning)
ConsSlower training than non-recurrent models · replaced as SOTA by Transformers

3 · Why bidirectional? The "river bank" demo

Analogy. Someone reads you a sentence one word at a time and asks you to commit to each word's role before hearing the next. You'd get some wrong, and you'd know why: you hadn't heard the end yet. A bidirectional model gets to hear the whole thing, twice, from both ends.
LSTM gates. An LSTM solves the vanishing gradient problem of simple RNNs using gates: Input, Forget, Output. That is the mechanism that lets it carry the word river forward far enough to matter.

4 · Why a CRF? Greedy vs structured

Analogy. Filling in a crossword by writing whatever fits each clue best, one at a time, and never checking whether the letters cross correctly. You'd end up with locally plausible words that can't coexist. The CRF layer is the step that checks the crossings.

The Bi-LSTM gives an excellent score for each word independently. Suppose those per-word bests are:

exp( score of the TRUE tag path ) P(y | x) = --------------------------------------- SUM over ALL possible paths of exp( score ) Goal: MAXIMISE the numerator (score of the true path) relative to ALL possible paths.

Same instinct as Viterbi in Chapter 6 — optimise the whole sequence, not each position greedily. The CRF learns constraints such as "a Verb is unlikely to follow a Determiner".

5 · The Transformer — self-attention

Analogy. An LSTM is a game of telephone — information about word 1 reaches word 20 only by being whispered through 19 intermediaries, degrading on the way. Self-attention is a conference call — every word hears every other word directly, at full volume, in one step.

Key idea: replaces recurrence (RNN/LSTM) with the self-attention mechanism.

The one-line contrast to memorise. An LSTM's path length between two words grows with their distance; a Transformer's is constant — 1 hop for every pair. That is why it beats recurrence on long-range dependencies and is far more parallelizable.

Tie it back to Chapter 2. These are contextual embeddings — bank gets a different vector in river bank and money bank. That is precisely why the lexical ambiguity of Chapter 1 largely stops being a problem here.

7 · Prompting and LLM agents

Prompting — no fine-tuning at all

"Tag the following sentence using the Penn Treebank tagset. Output only the word/tag pairs. Sentence: The concert was great."

Zero-shot / few-shot learning: the LLM can tag a sentence accurately without being explicitly fine-tuned on any POS corpus, based solely on its general pre-training.

The shift to notice. With BERT you still needed a labelled POS corpus and a training run. With prompting you need neither — only a well-written instruction. That is the entire argument of Chapter 8.

Conclusion — is modern POS tagging solved?

  • The task has achieved near-human performance thanks to deep learning.
  • Current SOTA: fine-tuned Transformer models (BERT / RoBERTa).
  • Future trend (LLMs): POS tagging is becoming an internal utility inside larger LLM agent systems.
  • The focus shifts from how to build the best tagger to how to use tags to power complex downstream AI systems.
