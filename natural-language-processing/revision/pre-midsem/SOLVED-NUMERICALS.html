Q1 — Feb 2026 (regular)

[2 + 3 = 5 Marks]

You are developing a text completion system and want to compare a statistical Bigram model against a simple Feedforward Neural Language Model (FFNN-LM). The training data consists of only these three sentences:

<s> deep learning </s>
<s> machine learning </s>
<s> deep thought </s>

You need to predict the next word following the context word "deep".

Part A [2 Marks]: Calculate the Laplace Smoothed probability of the word "learning" occurring after "deep". (Assume the Vocabulary V consists of all unique words in the corpus, including <s> and </s>.)

Part B [3 Marks]: Model architecture — Input: word embedding for "deep" x = [0.4, -0.2]. Hidden layer: 1 neuron with ReLU. Weights w = [0.5, -0.5], bias b = 0.1. Output layer computes unnormalized scores: z(learning) = 2*h, z(thought) = 0.5*h. Perform the forward pass to calculate the final Softmax probability for "learning".

Solution — Part A [2 marks]

STEP 1  Identify the vocabulary V                              (0.5 marks)

        Unique tokens, INCLUDING <s> and </s>:
            <s> , </s> , deep , learning , machine , thought

            V = 6

STEP 2  Get the counts                                         (0.5 marks)

        count("deep learning") = 1      (sentence 1 only)
        count("deep")          = 2      (sentences 1 and 3)

STEP 3  Substitute into the Laplace formula                    (0.5 marks)

                    count(deep learning) + 1        1 + 1
        P  =  ------------------------------  =  ---------
                    count(deep) + V                2 + 6

STEP 4  Final answer                                           (0.5 marks)

        P  =  2 / 8  =  0.25      (or 1/4)

Compare with unsmoothed MLE: 1/2 = 0.5. Laplace halved it, because it had to give probability mass to the four words that never follow deep.

Solution — Part B [3 marks]

STEP 1  Pre-activation (the weighted sum)                      (1.0 mark)

        z = (w1 × x1) + (w2 × x2) + b
          = (0.5 × 0.4) + (-0.5 × -0.2) + 0.1
          = 0.20 + 0.10 + 0.10
          = 0.40
                        ^^^^ note: negative × negative = POSITIVE

STEP 2  Apply ReLU                                             (0.5 marks)

        h = ReLU(0.4) = max(0.4, 0) = 0.4

STEP 3  Output layer scores                                    (0.5 marks)

        z(learning) = 2   × 0.4 = 0.8
        z(thought)  = 0.5 × 0.4 = 0.2

STEP 4  Softmax                                                (1.0 mark)

        e^0.8 = 2.2255
        e^0.2 = 1.2214
        sum   = 3.4469

                          e^0.8            2.2255
        P(learning) = ------------- = ------------- = 0.6457
                      e^0.8 + e^0.2      3.4469

        P(learning) ≈ 0.65

(Official key: 0.4 / 0.4 / 0.8 and 0.2 / ≈0.645 — all confirmed.)

Q1 — Mar 2026 (makeup)

[2 + 3 = 5 Marks]

Comparing a Bigram model against an FFNN-LM for a weather prediction system.

<s> sun hot </s>
<s> rain cold </s>
<s> sun bright </s>

Predict the next word following "sun".

Part A [2 Marks]: Calculate the probability of "bright" occurring after "sun". (Assume V consists of all unique words in the corpus, including <s> and </s>.)

Part B [3 Marks]: Input x = [0.5, 1.0]. Hidden layer: 1 neuron with Sigmoid. Weights w = [0.2, 0.4], bias b = -0.5. z(bright) = 1.5*h, z(hot) = 0.5*h. Find the softmax probability of "bright".

Solution — Part A [2 marks]

Vocabulary:  <s> , </s> , sun , hot , rain , cold , bright     ->  V = 7

Counts:      count("sun bright") = 1        count("sun") = 2

UNSMOOTHED (MLE):
        P = 1 / 2 = 0.50

LAPLACE (add-1):
             1 + 1        2
        P = -------- = ------- = 0.2222
             2 + 7        9

⚠️ The question is ambiguous here. Unlike the Feb paper it does not say the word "Laplace" — but it does hand you V and tell you to include <s>/</s>, which is only needed for smoothing. Give the Laplace answer (0.2222) as your main one, show the MLE (0.5) in one line, and state your assumption. That covers you either way, and "state assumptions clearly" is printed on the front of your exam paper.

Solution — Part B [3 marks]

STEP 1  z = (0.2 × 0.5) + (0.4 × 1.0) + (-0.5)
          = 0.10 + 0.40 - 0.50
          = 0.00

STEP 2  h = sigmoid(0) = 1 / (1 + e^0) = 1 / (1 + 1) = 0.5
                                                        ^^^ sigmoid(0) is ALWAYS 0.5

STEP 3  z(bright) = 1.5 × 0.5 = 0.75
        z(hot)    = 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.25

STEP 4  e^0.75 = 2.1170
        e^0.25 = 1.2840
        sum    = 3.4010

        P(bright) = 2.1170 / 3.4010 = 0.6225   ≈ 0.62

Q2 — Feb 2026 (regular)

[3 + 2 = 5 Marks]

(A) An HMM-based POS tagger uses the following probabilities.

| Current / Next | DT | NN | VB | |---|---|---|---| | START | 0.4 | 0.4 | 0.2 | | DT | 0.2 | 0.7 | 0.1 | | NN | 0.1 | 0.3 | 0.6 | | VB | 0.5 | 0.4 | 0.1 |

For the phrase "a dog barks", determine the most likely sequence of POS tags using the Viterbi algorithm. Show all intermediate calculations, including initialization, recursion and termination. [3 Marks]

(B) A speech recognition system uses an HMM with states Vowel (V), Consonant (C), Silence (S). π(V)=0.5, π(C)=0.3, π(S)=0.2.

3. Recursion — word 3 = "barks"

Solution — (B) Forward [2 marks]

alpha1 (after "ah")  =  pi(state) × P(ah|state)

    V:  0.5 × 0.8  =  0.40
    C:  0.3 × 0.1  =  0.03
    S:  0.2 × 0.1  =  0.02

alpha2 (after "sh")  =  [ SUM over i of alpha1(i) × a(i->j) ] × P(sh|j)
                          ^^^ SUM, because this is FORWARD, not Viterbi

    V:  [0.40×0.6 + 0.03×0.4 + 0.02×0.3] × 0.1
      = [0.240 + 0.012 + 0.006] × 0.1  =  0.258 × 0.1  =  0.0258

    C:  [0.40×0.3 + 0.03×0.4 + 0.02×0.3] × 0.7
      = [0.120 + 0.012 + 0.006] × 0.7  =  0.138 × 0.7  =  0.0966

    S:  [0.40×0.1 + 0.03×0.2 + 0.02×0.4] × 0.2
      = [0.040 + 0.006 + 0.008] × 0.2  =  0.054 × 0.2  =  0.0108

P(O)  =  0.0258 + 0.0966 + 0.0108  =  0.1332

POSTERIOR  =  each alpha2 divided by the total:

    P(V2 | O)  =  0.0258 / 0.1332  =  0.194
    P(C2 | O)  =  0.0966 / 0.1332  =  0.725     <- highest
    P(S2 | O)  =  0.0108 / 0.1332  =  0.081

Most likely state at time 2: Consonant (C).

Why: the observation "sh" has a very high emission probability from the Consonant state (0.7), and sufficient probability mass transitions into C from the earlier states.

Solution — (B) Forward + prediction [2 marks]

alpha1 (after "ah"):
    V:  0.2 × 0.4  =  0.08
    C:  0.5 × 0.4  =  0.20
    N:  0.3 × 0.2  =  0.06

alpha2 (after "sh"):
    V:  [0.08×0.1 + 0.20×0.6 + 0.06×0.3] × 0.2
      = [0.008 + 0.120 + 0.018] × 0.2 = 0.146 × 0.2 = 0.0292

    C:  [0.08×0.7 + 0.20×0.2 + 0.06×0.3] × 0.2
      = [0.056 + 0.040 + 0.018] × 0.2 = 0.114 × 0.2 = 0.0228

    N:  [0.08×0.2 + 0.20×0.2 + 0.06×0.4] × 0.6
      = [0.016 + 0.040 + 0.024] × 0.6 = 0.080 × 0.6 = 0.0480

Total = 0.0292 + 0.0228 + 0.0480 = 0.1000        (conveniently exactly 0.1)

FILTERED POSTERIOR  P(q2 | o1,o2):
    V:  0.0292 / 0.1  =  0.292
    C:  0.0228 / 0.1  =  0.228
    N:  0.0480 / 0.1  =  0.480      <- most likely at t=2

One-step prediction — no new forward computation needed. Just push the posterior through the transition matrix:

P(q3 = j | o1,o2)  =  SUM over i of  P(q2 = i | o1,o2) × a(i -> j)

V:  0.292×0.1 + 0.228×0.6 + 0.480×0.3
  = 0.0292 + 0.1368 + 0.1440  =  0.310

C:  0.292×0.7 + 0.228×0.2 + 0.480×0.3
  = 0.2044 + 0.0456 + 0.1440  =  0.394      <- most likely at t=3

N:  0.292×0.2 + 0.228×0.2 + 0.480×0.4
  = 0.0584 + 0.0456 + 0.1920  =  0.296

    P(q3 | o1,o2)  =  ( V = 0.310 , C = 0.394 , N = 0.296 )
    check: 0.310 + 0.394 + 0.296 = 1.000  ✓

The trick this question tests: prediction = take the posterior you already have and multiply by the transition matrix. No emission is involved, because you haven't observed the next symbol yet.

Q1/Q2 — Oct 2024 (regular)

Q1 [5 Marks] — Forward algorithm

Emission probabilities:

| Word | Noun | Verb | Adj | |---|---|---|---| | "cat" | 0.9 | – | 0.1 | | "jumps" | 0.3 | 0.7 | – | | "quickly" | – | 0.3 | 0.7 |

Transitions: Noun→Noun 0.5, Noun→Verb 0.5, Verb→Verb 0.7, Verb→Adj 0.3. Using the forward algorithm, calculate the probability of the observation sequence "cat jumps quickly".

Solution

1. Initialization
   Initial state equally likely for Noun or Verb  ->  P = 1/2 each
   (state this assumption — the question doesn't give initial probabilities)

2. Forward step 1 — "cat"
   P(Noun) = 1/2 × 0.9 = 0.45
   Verb is not an option: the emission for "cat" as a Verb is "–" (zero)

3. Forward step 2 — "jumps"
   P(Verb) = P(Noun) × P(Noun→Verb) × P(jumps|Verb)
           = 0.45 × 0.5 × 0.7
           = 0.1575

4. Forward step 3 — "quickly"
   P(Adj)  = P(Verb) × P(Verb→Adj) × P(quickly|Adj)
           = 0.1575 × 0.3 × 0.7
           = 0.033075

5. Conclusion
   P("cat jumps quickly") = 0.0331

Only one path survives, because every other route hits a – (zero emission). That's why the sums collapse to single terms here.

Q2 [5 Marks] — Forward algorithm ⚠️ official answer contains an error

| Transitions | S1 | S2 | | Emissions | S1 | S2 | |---|---|---|---|---|---|---| | S1 | 0.7 | 0.3 | | "X" | 0.6 | 0.4 | | S2 | 0.6 | 0.4 | | "Y" | 0.3 | 0.7 |

Use the Forward algorithm for the sequence "X Y", with initial probabilities P(S1)=0.6, P(S2)=0.4.

Solution

1. Initialization (observation "X")
   alpha1(S1) = 0.6 × 0.6 = 0.36
   alpha1(S2) = 0.4 × 0.4 = 0.16

2. Forward step (observation "Y")

   alpha2(S1) = [ alpha1(S1) × a(S1->S1)  +  alpha1(S2) × a(S2->S1) ] × P(Y|S1)
              = [ 0.36 × 0.7  +  0.16 × 0.6 ] × 0.3
              = [ 0.252 + 0.096 ] × 0.3
              = 0.348 × 0.3  =  0.1044

   alpha2(S2) = [ alpha1(S1) × a(S1->S2)  +  alpha1(S2) × a(S2->S2) ] × P(Y|S2)
              = [ 0.36 × 0.3  +  0.16 × 0.4 ] × 0.7
              = [ 0.108 + 0.064 ] × 0.7
              = 0.172 × 0.7  =  0.1204

3. Total
   P("X Y") = 0.1044 + 0.1204 = 0.2248

⚠️ Read this — the printed answer key is wrong

The official solution writes:

P(S1) = (0.36 × 0.7 × 0.3) + (0.16 × 0.4 × 0.3) = 0.0756
P(S2) = (0.36 × 0.3 × 0.7) + (0.16 × 0.6 × 0.7) = 0.1032

It used a(S2→S2) = 0.4 where it needed a(S2→S1) = 0.6, and a(S2→S1) = 0.6 where it needed a(S2→S2) = 0.4 — the two second terms are swapped. Its printed totals don't even match its own arithmetic.

Use the method above, not those numbers. To reach state j, you sum over every predecessor i using a(i → j) — the arrow must point at the state you are computing. If a marker challenges you, that one sentence is your defence.

| Transitions | Noun | Verb | Adj | | Emissions | Noun | Verb | Adj | |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---| | Noun | 0.4 | 0.3 | 0.3 | | "run" | 0.8 | 0.2 | – | | Verb | 0.1 | 0.8 | 0.1 | | "fast" | 0.6 | – | 0.4 | | Adj | 0.6 | 0.3 | 0.1 | | "dog" | 0.7 | 0.3 | – |

Find the most likely POS tag sequence for "run fast" using Viterbi.

Solution

No initial probabilities given  ->  assume equal, 1/3 each. STATE THIS.

t = 1  ("run")

    V(Verb) = 1/3 × 0.2 = 0.0667

t = 2  ("fast")

    For Noun:   from Noun  0.2667 × 0.4 × 0.6 = 0.0640    <- max
                from Verb  0.0667 × 0.1 × 0.6 = 0.0040
                from Adj   0      × 0.6 × 0.6 = 0
                => 0.0640, backpointer Noun

    For Verb:   emission P(fast|Verb) is "–" = 0
                => every path is 0. Verb is impossible here.

    For Adj:    from Noun  0.2667 × 0.3 × 0.4 = 0.0320    <- max
                from Verb  0.0667 × 0.1 × 0.4 = 0.0027
                from Adj   0                  = 0
                => 0.0320, backpointer Noun

Highest final value: Noun (0.0640), ahead of Adj (0.0320).
Backtracking gives Noun at t=1 too.

Q2 [5 Marks] — Viterbi

| Transitions | S1 | S2 | | Emissions | S1 | S2 | |---|---|---|---|---|---|---| | S1 | 0.6 | 0.4 | | "A" | 0.5 | 0.5 | | S2 | 0.3 | 0.7 | | "B" | 0.4 | 0.6 |

Using Viterbi, calculate the most probable hidden state sequence for the observation sequence "A B".

Solution

STEP 1  Initialization ("A") — initial probabilities assumed equal (0.5 each)

    P(S1) = 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.25
    P(S2) = 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.25

STEP 2  Recursion ("B") — take the MAX path into each state

    For S1:  max( 0.25 × 0.6 , 0.25 × 0.3 ) × 0.4
           = max( 0.150 , 0.075 ) × 0.4
           = 0.150 × 0.4  =  0.06            backpointer: S1

    For S2:  max( 0.25 × 0.4 , 0.25 × 0.7 ) × 0.6
           = max( 0.100 , 0.175 ) × 0.6
           = 0.175 × 0.6  =  0.105           backpointer: S2

STEP 3  Termination

    P(S2) = 0.105  >  P(S1) = 0.06
    => the most probable final state is S2, and its backpointer is S2

ANSWER:   S2  S2

P2 — Forward pass with ReLU [3 marks]

Input x = [1.0, -0.5], weights w = [0.3, 0.4], bias b = 0.1, ReLU. Scores: z(A) = 3*h, z(B) = 1*h. Find P(A). (Given e^0.3 = 1.3499, e^0.1 = 1.1052.)

Answer P2 
z = (0.3 × 1.0) + (0.4 × -0.5) + 0.1
  = 0.30 - 0.20 + 0.10
  = 0.20

h = ReLU(0.2) = 0.2

z(A) = 3 × 0.2 = 0.6
z(B) = 1 × 0.2 = 0.2

e^0.6 = 1.8221     e^0.2 = 1.2214     sum = 3.0435

P(A) = 1.8221 / 3.0435 = 0.5987  ≈ 0.60

(The e-values in the question were a distractor — you need e^0.6 and e^0.2. In the exam, if a needed value isn't given, compute it or leave the fraction symbolically and say so.)

P3 — Forward pass with a negative z [2 marks]

Input x = [0.2, 0.1], weights w = [0.5, -1.0], bias b = -0.3, ReLU. Scores z(A) = 2*h, z(B) = 4*h. Find P(A).

Answer P3 
z = (0.5 × 0.2) + (-1.0 × 0.1) + (-0.3)
  = 0.10 - 0.10 - 0.30
  = -0.30

h = ReLU(-0.3) = max(-0.3, 0) = 0        <-- the neuron is DEAD

z(A) = 2 × 0 = 0        z(B) = 4 × 0 = 0

P(A) = e^0 / (e^0 + e^0) = 1 / 2 = 0.5

The lesson: when ReLU kills the neuron, both scores are 0 and softmax returns exactly 0.5 — the model has no opinion. If you get 0.5, check whether z went negative. That's a real answer here, not a mistake.

P4 — Viterbi [3 marks]
