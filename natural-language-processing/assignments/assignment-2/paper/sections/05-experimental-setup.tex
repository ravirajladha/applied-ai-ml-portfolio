\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Model and training}

We fine-tune \texttt{t5-small} \cite{raffel2020t5}, a 60M-parameter
encoder--decoder. T5 is trained as a multi-task text-to-text model, so every
input must declare its task; we prepend the standard \texttt{"summarize: "}
prefix. Hyperparameters are given in Table~\ref{tab:hyper}.

\begin{table}[t]
\caption{Training and decoding configuration.}
\label{tab:hyper}
\centering
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
\textbf{Setting} & \textbf{Value} \\
\midrule
Base model              & \texttt{t5-small} (60M) \\
Task prefix             & \texttt{"summarize: "} \\
Training examples       & 4{,}000 \\
Epochs                  & 1 \\
Batch size              & 8 \\
Learning rate           & $3\times10^{-4}$ \\
Max input (training)    & 128 tokens \\
Max input (inference)   & 256 tokens \\
Max target              & 32 tokens \\
Beam size               & 4 \\
No-repeat $n$-gram      & 2 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Two choices deserve comment. First, the input is truncated harder during
training (128 tokens) than at inference (256). Attention cost grows with the
square of sequence length and this model was trained on CPU alone, so halving
the training sequence roughly halves the cost per example. The median review is
74 tokens, and reviews tend to state their verdict early, so the truncation
discards little of what the target depends on. Second, decoding blocks repeated
bigrams; without it the model is prone to degenerate repetition
(\emph{``good good good''}) on short targets.

\textbf{Hardware and runtime.} Training ran on CPU only, with no GPU available.
We deliberately do not report a wall-clock figure: the run was not instrumented
and no training-state record survives, so the two informal timings recorded in
our own project notes (approximately 50 and approximately 112 minutes)
cannot be reconciled after the fact. What matters for the argument is only that
the entire budget was a single epoch over 4{,}000 examples on commodity CPU
hardware --- the regime a student or a small team actually works in.

\subsection{Baselines}

We compare against two baselines, each chosen to answer a specific question.

\textbf{Lead-1} returns the first sentence of the review verbatim. It asks
whether the task is trivially extractive. It is a deceptively strong baseline
here, because customers frequently reuse their opening words in their headline.

\textbf{Un-tuned \texttt{t5-small}} is the same pretrained checkpoint with the
same prompt and decoding settings, but no fine-tuning. It isolates the
contribution of in-domain fine-tuning from the contribution of pretraining.

\subsection{Metric}

We report ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2 and ROUGE-L \cite{lin2004rouge} against the
customer-written headline, computed with the HuggingFace \texttt{evaluate}
implementation over the 300 held-out reviews. We report no significance test;
Section~\ref{sec:limitations} states what that costs us.
