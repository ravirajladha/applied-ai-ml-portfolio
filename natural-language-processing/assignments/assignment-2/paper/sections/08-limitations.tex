\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

We set these out in full, because several of them bear directly on how far the
claims of Section~\ref{sec:analysis} can be carried.

\textbf{No from-scratch baseline.} We implemented a recurrent encoder--decoder
with additive attention \cite{bahdanau2015attention} and verified that it
learns, but never trained it on the full data. The paper therefore contains no
comparison between a model trained from scratch and a pretrained one adapted,
which is the comparison that would establish how much of the fine-tuned model's
performance is owed to pretraining. This is the single largest gap.

\textbf{No significance testing.} We report point estimates from one run. The
differences we discuss are large relative to the values involved and consistent
in direction, but we did not test them, and the three ROUGE variants are
correlated, so their agreement is weaker evidence than three independent
measurements would be.

\textbf{No human evaluation.} As Section~\ref{sec:rouge-limits} concedes, if
ROUGE substantially measures form-fit then it cannot tell us whether the
fine-tuned summaries are more useful to a reader --- only that they match the
reference register. Establishing usefulness requires human judgement, or at
minimum an embedding-based metric \cite{zhang2020bertscore}. We ran neither.

\textbf{A single configuration.} One dataset, one product domain, one model
size, one seed, one epoch, 4{,}000 training examples. We do not know how much
of the fine-tuning gain is available at 1{,}000 examples or how much more
remains at 40{,}000, and the form-fit account predicts the curve should
saturate early --- register is a cheap thing to learn --- but we did not
measure it.

\textbf{The evaluation-size observation is anecdotal.} The difference between
0.234 on 24 reviews and 0.174 on 300 was noticed in passing, not produced by a
controlled sweep over evaluation-set sizes. It is consistent with the
coarseness argument of Section~\ref{sec:rouge-limits}, and it should be treated
as motivation for that argument rather than as evidence for it.

\textbf{The aggregation ceiling is raised, not removed.} As
Section~\ref{sec:method} describes, the reduce step re-enters the same
256-token encoder, so it saturates at roughly forty to fifty per-review
summaries. The demonstration uses sixty reviews and therefore sits at the edge
of that regime; a hierarchical reduce would remove the limit and was not
implemented.

\textbf{The aspect miner is a heuristic.} It is frequency-and-polarity based,
not a trained aspect-based sentiment model. It cannot resolve a negated aspect
inside an otherwise positive sentence, and it collapses singular and plural
forms within each list but not across them, which is why \emph{formula} and
\emph{formulas} head opposite lists in Section~\ref{sec:analysis}.

\textbf{The displayed verdict is partly templated.} The paragraph the
application presents combines a threshold-selected mood phrase, the ranked
aspect lists and one generated sentence. The neural model contributes the
summaries and that sentence; the surrounding structure is fixed. We describe it
this way in Section~\ref{sec:method} to avoid implying end-to-end generation.

\textbf{Training cost was not instrumented.} No timing record survives the
training run, so we report the hardware and the budget in examples and epochs
but no wall-clock figure.
